# Design

## Use revisions as the spine, not contributions

Both tables have a primary key on `(source, plan, revision)`. Ordinary reports write one row to each. `Store.cancelPlan` writes a revision with the fixed user-cancel actor but **no** contribution. A contribution offset is therefore not a revision offset. Starting from contributions, zipping two arrays by position, or treating every cancelled lifecycle as a user action would misattribute history.

In one read transaction, seek a descending window of revision keys for the selected plan and left-join contributions on the full key. Project only metadata. For a report, take its time from the contribution: automatic binding can advance the revision while preserving the prior plan-level `reportedAt`. For a confirmed user cancellation, use the History actor predicate and the revision's stored `reportedAt`. Never return raw actor JSON, snapshots, file paths or user content. There is no need to read capture bytes, request host sessions/messages, diff snapshots or change the writer.

The contribution item IDs are the tasks **named in that report**, not a proof of all structural or status differences. Keep all IDs as written, including ones no longer on the plan. A valid empty list means “no task IDs reported”; start, link and revise can legitimately record that. Don't look up present-day titles as historical names. If an ordinary revision has no corresponding contribution, keep its revision and recorded plan time, qualify authorship/task details as unavailable, and do not borrow attribution. Invalid data must not produce a plausible report.

## One bounded read on demand

Announce “Plan activity (additive revision 1)” in the shared contract first. The producer and unconnected guest reader/renderer can be developed against that contract in parallel; accept the service form before activating the guest reader, then connect the rail and board List consumers in turn. This preserves the repository's consumer-switch rule without changing protocol/schema 2.

Extend authenticated `GET /v1/history` with one distinct form: `?source=S&plan=P&through=N`. `through` is a positive safe-integer revision, inclusive. Do not accept `cursor` or `limit` in this form. Without `through`, keep the existing oldest-first row-offset API, payload and limits unchanged; ordinary History pays nothing for the join.

Return one page of at most 20 consecutive revisions (the Store's normal page size), newest first, ending no lower than 1. The reply identifies protocol/schema 2, store, source binding, source, plan and cleanup generation, and includes rows and `nextThrough` (the revision immediately before the oldest row, or null at revision 1). Each row carries revision and time, and either the report's mode, reporter session, attributed session and item IDs, the fixed user discriminator, or an explicit unavailable report. Match contribution identity to the revision; take cancellation solely from the stored actor, not from a missing join. Use named fields, existing identifier/size checks and the service's response ceiling. Read indexed keys directly; do not scan older history to skip to a page, fetch a plan detail, or materialize full snapshots in the service process. Refuse an impossible boundary or gap rather than presenting a shorter page as complete.

Pin the first `through` to the listed revision at expansion. Older uses `nextThrough`; Newer reverses the window within the same pin. Replace the displayed page rather than accumulating rows, disable duplicate in-flight page actions, and leave the rest of List interactive. A newer report does not move the page under the reader; indicate when a newer revision is listed and let reopening start at that revision. Routine repaints retain the disclosure/page without another read. Closing, leaving List, hiding/disposal, or changing reading identity (including source, store/binding, account, plan and cleanup generation) retires pending work. Validate reply identity, contiguous revisions and continuation before display. Keep failures and explicit retry local to the section; no automatic retries or polling.

**Why not just reuse full detail or call the existing History loader?** Detail follows contribution and task pages together until complete or capped; even a one-row activity page would then depend on the entire history. The existing History loader takes a suffix of up to five pages, has no contribution records, and stops at 250 revisions. Neither is a suitable pager. The explicit query avoids changing list refreshes, adding a route or negotiating a new service capability. Older services reject unknown query keys with 404: show activity unavailable on that explicit read, not “no reports” or “plan deleted”. Legacy guests make no such request. Archived or recording-off plans remain readable under existing read rules; rollback needs no data conversion.

## Reuse the two existing presentations

Mount one List disclosure on rail and board independently of complete task detail. Do not edit the switch, its decision logic or its handlers. Share the small revision-number, time and user-cancellation rendering with History; keep its transition labels, truncation note, digest and review checkpoints there. Do not mount a second History panel in List. Troubleshooting retains its existing diagnostic authorship.

Use the task rail's localized attribution phrases, **“reported directly by {reporter}”** and **“reported by {reporter} for {attributed}”**. Resolve session names with the same verified-source/recorded-session fallback. Distinguish the plan coordinator by the plan owner ID, not by a display name or the currently selected session; a direct child report names the reporting sub-agent, and a relay names the actual reporter and credited child. If needed, show the role alongside the phrase, not in place of either session. For the fixed user row use the existing **“Cancelled by user”** translation without an agent or changed tasks. These are records of reports, not claims about who executed work.

Use keyboard-operable disclosure and paging controls with accessible names, readable times, wrapping item IDs and meaningful focus after paging. Translate section, controls, roles, state and error text in all three interface languages; do not translate recorded IDs or names. No saved preference.
